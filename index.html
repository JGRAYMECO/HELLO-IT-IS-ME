Big Tech, power and democratic governance.

Joanne E. Gray is an Associate Professor of Media and Communications at the University of Sydney and Editor-in-Chief of Policy & Internet. Her research examines how global technology companies acquire and exercise power, and what this means for democratic governance.

Working across political economy, law and public policy, she studies Big Tech, digital sovereignty, AI infrastructure, platform regulation and emerging technologies. Her research has informed Australian technology policy and regulation, including work on online safety, privacy and platform accountability.

Research

Research at the intersection of technology and power.

Examining how technology companies shape public life, how digital technologies are regulated, and how states can hold concentrated private power to account.

  • Big Tech

    Big Tech and private power

    How technology companies set rules for markets, information and public life—and the implications for democratic accountability.

  • Infrastructure

    Digital sovereignty and AI infrastructure

    How dependence on cloud services, computing and AI systems shapes a country’s capacity to govern.

  • Regulation

    Platform governance and regulation

    How platforms govern—and are governed—through algorithms, moderation, competition, copyright and public policy.

  • Emerging tech

    Emerging technologies and the public interest

    Privacy, consent and safety in immersive technologies, smart glasses and AI-enabled wearables.
