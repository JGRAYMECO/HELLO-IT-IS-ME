Platform Governance and Regulation

Digital platforms make and enforce rules for billions of people through their terms of service, content moderation, recommendation algorithms and automated enforcement systems. My research examines how platforms exercise this governing power, how users experience and respond to it, and how governments can regulate it in the public interest.

What is platform governance?

Platform governance has two connected meanings. It describes the rules and systems through which platforms govern their users: what can be posted, what is promoted or suppressed, and how breaches are detected and punished. It also describes the laws, regulators and institutions through which states govern platforms. My work studies both, and the relationship between them.

This research began with copyright, one of the first areas in which platforms built large-scale private enforcement systems, and my book Google Rules (Oxford University Press, 2020) traced how Google came to shape copyright law and practice. It has since extended to content moderation, recommendation algorithms, misinformation, online harms and generative AI. It also underpins my argument in Big Tech Dynasties about the private governing power of the largest technology companies.

Content moderation and its circumvention

Platforms increasingly limit the presence, spread and amplification of harmful content. Users respond by adapting. With Rachel Gillett and D. Bondy Valdovinos Kaye, I investigated cultures of content moderation circumvention among Facebook users, the everyday tactics people use to evade platform rules. It relates to an earlier research project I led on user strategies for circumventing content moderation policies, funded through Facebook’s Content Governance research scheme (Queensland University of Technology Digital Media Research Centre, 2020–2021).

Algorithms and recommendation

Recommendation systems decide much of what people see online. With Ariadna Matamoros-Fernández and colleagues, I studied how YouTube’s “up next” feature recommends videos across different issues and over time. I am a Chief Investigator on the ARC Discovery Project Australian Experiences of Algorithmic Culture on TikTok (the For You Project), where I lead the platform governance dimensions of the research. Related work examines the politics of “diversity” in cultural recommender systems, shared cultural experience on highly personalised platforms, and the practices ordinary users employ to shape the content they are served.

Copyright and automated enforcement

Copyright enforcement on platforms is largely automated. With Nicolas Suzor, I used machine learning to study automated copyright enforcement on YouTube at scale. With D. Bondy Valdovinos Kaye, I analysed how YouTube creators understand and talk about copyright. With Cheryl Foong, I traced the development of Australia’s copyright site-blocking regime. Most recently, with Kaye and Kylie Pappalardo, I have examined the industry, creator and legal implications of generative AI copying artistic style.

Misinformation and information retrieval

Platforms shape what counts as reliable information. With colleagues, I have studied YouTube comments on COVID-19 news videos featuring Bill Gates, where conspiracy theories flourished. With Olga Boichak and colleagues, I have examined how responsibility for information should be assessed as search moves from autocomplete to generative AI systems such as ChatGPT.

Online harms and their regulation

Regulating online harms raises difficult questions about whose norms are applied, and to whom. With Milica Stilinovic and Jonathon Hutchinson, I have drawn on the international doctrine of the responsibility to protect to examine the Western-centricity of online harms regulation. I also contribute to interdisciplinary work on online safety research across human–computer interaction, media studies and policy. My research on immersive and wearable technologies, including smart glasses and privacy, extends this work to emerging platforms.

Platform regulation in Australia

Australia has become one of the most active jurisdictions in platform regulation, through the News Media Bargaining Code, the ACCC’s Digital Platform Services Inquiry, online safety legislation and the social media minimum age. My research has informed this agenda:

  • My work informed Australia’s social media minimum age legislation, and is cited by regulators including the eSafety Commissioner and the ACCC.
  • Submissions to the ACCC’s Digital Platform Services Inquiry (2021), the Australian Government’s Copyright Enforcement Review (2023, with D. Bondy Valdovinos Kaye), the Safe and Responsible AI in Australia consultation (2023, with colleagues) and the Joint Select Committee on Social Media and Australian Society (2024, with colleagues).
  • Advice to Meta, ByteDance, Microsoft and YouTube on platform governance, including work that led YouTube to remove medical misinformation during the COVID-19 pandemic.

As Editor-in-Chief of Policy & Internet, I also work with researchers worldwide on internet policy and platform regulation.
